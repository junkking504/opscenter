Before you say yes

Questions for Waste Harmonics, and what to set up now

Ask the broker

  1. The confirmed store list. Exact addresses, banners and store numbers under contract in your area (they said 17). That replaces the "verify" rows above and tells you whether Winn-Dixie is even in play after its Louisiana exit.
  2. What a "load" is. Volume, weight, or per-ton with tickets? Is $1,200 all-in (dump fees included) or plus disposal? Minimums, partial loads, standby/show-up fee, after-hours and weekend rates.
  3. Trigger and authorization. Who issues the work order (store manager vs. broker ticket), how fast after power loss, and who signs off scope on site. Hess (their founder) has said store-level communication is the weak point in these events.
  4. Payment terms and who pays. Brokers commonly pay net 45–90. Confirm terms, invoice documentation (photos, weight tickets, store sign-off) and whether Aldi or the broker is the payor.
  5. Insurance and onboarding. COI limits (GL/auto/WC, sometimes pollution), W-9, vendor portal, background checks. Do this in October, not during a watch.
  6. Exclusivity and competition. Are you the primary for these stores or one of several subs? What volume did they move in Ida and Francine, and with whom?
  7. Non-storm work. Fixture removal and debris on Aldi's remaining conversions, routine bulky pickups, compactor overflow. Recurring revenue from the same relationship.
  8. Junk King corporate. Check whether Waste Harmonics is already a Junk King national account. If so, corporate may route the work and take the key-account admin fee, and refusing a key-account job carries a penalty under the 2026 FDD.

Set up before the next watch

  1. Landfill agreements. Accounts and emergency-hours contacts at two landfills per market (see ▲ on the map). Confirm they take putrescible food waste after a storm; some require it bagged or lined.
  2. Fuel. Post-storm fuel is the real choke point. Fleet cards that work out of market, a reserve plan, and a rule to top every truck when a watch is issued.
  3. Staging. If NOLA is in the cone, move trucks to Baton Rouge or Hammond before landfall; crews can't work from a flooded yard. Keep one truck outside the cone entirely.
  4. Crew roster and pay. Who is available after a storm, storm-rate pay, housing/fuel stipends. Assume a third of the team has their own damage.
  5. PPE and gear. Nitrile gloves, N95s, Tyvek, bed liners/tarps for leachate, shovels, bins. Rotting product is a biohazard and the trucks will stink for weeks; budget a steam-clean per truck.
  6. Documentation. Per-load photo (before/after cooler, loaded truck, landfill ticket) and a one-page store sign-off. Invoice within 24 hours. Brokers pay what they can document.
  7. Overflow partners. Line up two subcontract haulers or roll-off providers in each market. In this scenario the fleet finishes in the days shown above; the broker will expect faster.
  8. Sell direct. Take the prospect table above and sign standby rate sheets with Rouses, the NOLA independents and two distribution centers before June 1, 2027. The broker call proves there is demand; the direct relationships are where the margin is.
